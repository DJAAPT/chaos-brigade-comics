### Chapter 3 — The Unmarked Dead

Gulgrim sees them first, because Gulgrim is where dwarves belong: out front,
behind the bear, low to the ground and high on purpose. Two guards. Pinned to
the south gate wall with short swords through their chests, holding them upright
like specimens in a collection nobody ordered. Moonlight doing nothing for
anybody. The party stops the way parties stop behind dwarves. All at once.

And then James does the thing James does, which is investigate the corpses
before anyone finishes gasping. Investigation, advantage, sixteen. He checks the
wrists — of course he checks the wrists, the whole table has been trained like
dogs on wrist ink for a month — and looks up with the worst news in the session
so far. Clean. No tattoos. Neither of them. Whatever these men were, they were
not the marked city watch, and they were not the guild's extortion crew either,
because the guild signs its work and the watch is signed by the city. Two dead
men with swords in them and nothing on their arms, and every identification
system the party owns just failed at once.

Shade asks the only useful question: did Chip ever share the tattoo lore? And
Chip — who has been carrying the single most relevant fact in the party for two
sessions — says he told them nothing yet. Been too busy. The lorekeeper of the
guild ink has been sitting on it since the general store, through the apothecary,
through the festival, through the band, and mentions it now, at the corpses,
which is very Chip: the right information, at the worst time, delivered
cheerfully. The dirt path past the cobblestones gives them the rest of what the
dead men can't: heavy boot prints, more than one set, exact number indiscernible,
heading further south. Debate, brief and sharp: alert the city and risk
questioning, lose the trail, lose the girl — or pursue, now, into the dark, seven
shapes against unknown archers. They pursue. Of course they pursue. The butler
theories walk with them: recruit him, he's strong; prove to him we can protect
her; and Aaron's movie-logic special — maybe he faked the whole kidnapping to
teach them a lesson, took her home nicely, left the note to freak them out. "I
watch too many movies," Aaron says, which at this table counts as a rules
citation.

**DM NOTE — IDENTIFICATION.** *A month of wrist-checking, and the first bodies
have bare arms. I put those swords there. I left the wrists clean on purpose.
The table's entire forensic method just retired, and Gulgrim's sixteen is the
only reason they know it.*

### Chapter 4 — Dirty 20 and 22 Damage

Past the walls the road narrows into forest, a stream running alongside it like
a second road for people who don't need roads. The prints give out where the
ground goes soft and treacherous. Darkvision roll call, because half the party
is about to fight blind: Derek, sixty feet, elven eyes; everyone else, thirty,
torchlight and hope. Diamond formation, Rocketcandy spearheading out front, and
then the forest demonstrates the problem with formations, which is that they
assume the enemy will be where you are looking.

The first bolt comes for Keechwah. Dexterity save: dirty twenty — a twenty, total,
dodge with math on top. He steps
aside and the bolt buries itself in the dirt at his feet, and Aaron laughs the
laugh of a man whose number came up, because a dirty twenty is the game winking
at you. Keechwah strains for the shooter — perception three, natural two on the
die — and gets nothing but a quadrant. Top right. Somewhere. The party draws
weapons in the dark, a sound I have come to love the way other DMs love
thunder, and then the second bolt comes for Chip. Dexterity save: four. Ten
piercing plus twelve poison, twenty-two total, buried deep and high in the
shoulder. Chip goes down to a knee in the leaf mold with poison working through
him, and the rescue has its first casualty before it has a target, a direction,
or the faintest idea what is shooting at it. Twenty-two damage. Say it with me.
The night is young and the dice are already cruel.

**DM NOTE — TWENTY-TWO.** *Ten plus twelve. I rolled it in the open so Aaron
could watch. The warlock sidesteps on twenty; the druid eats twenty-two on
four. Same forest, same volley, eleven apart on the die. Nobody at the table
will ever convince me dice don't have personalities.*

### Chapter 5 — Aim for That Blob

Initiative gets rolled in the dark with everyone shouting numbers — turn order,
high goes first — and Vadania
goes first — eighteen — and does the disciplined thing: bow readied on
visibility, finger off the string until there is something to hit. Rules check,
confirmed, loaded gun placed on the table. Chip, bleeding poison in the dirt,
does the Chip thing instead: goes starry. Light blooms off him in ripples —
beautiful, conspicuous, tactically insane light, the kind of glow that says
*here I am* in a forest full of archers — and by it he reads his own wound like
a textbook. Perception sixteen: steep downward angle, shoulder high, very close
range, from the left. He moves. Heals himself eight. Files it. The scholar
bleeds neatly.

Shade melts thirty feet into a bush and holds. A bolt comes for Rocketcandy:
seventeen against eighteen armor — miss, dodge, the paladin's expensive training
paying for itself one point at a time. Water Bear sniffs the wind — nine, Keen
Sight re-roll, ten — and finds nothing, and I want you to sit with that for a
second, because the bear will matter enormously in about twenty minutes, and
right now he is useless, and the table has already started joking about it.
Keechwah, squinting into the black: perception thirteen, and the wound-depth
math says above, close, personal. He readies Witch Bolt on seeing anything at
all. The forest holds its breath. Everybody holds everything. Loaded guns,
everywhere, no targets.

So Rocketcandy walks to the biggest tree and yells up it. "Yo, anybody up
here?" Silence. "Then I'm gonna start chopping down the tree," he announces,
and I tell him — gently, because I love him — that as much as I appreciate his
faith in his ability to fell old growth in one swing, he cannot. He climbs
instead: ten feet up, half speed, armor clanking like a shopkeeper's bell, and
halfway up he looks higher and stops. Perception fourteen. Dark mass. Fifteen
feet above him in the thick foliage. "Barely fingertips touch," I tell him, and
the table leans in, because there is *something* up there, and then the
something moves: acrobatics twenty-two, a swing around the branch, and both feet
into the paladin's chest — six bludgeoning for the kick, six more for the fall,
Rocketcandy out of the canopy and prone in the dirt with the whole forest
laughing except the forest is trying to kill them.

And here — *here* — is where Gulgrim invents artillery. Thirty feet, a dagger,
a Light spell, Athletics ten with fresh Heroic Inspiration I hand him on the
spot for sheer audacity: the dagger sticks halfway up the trunk and blooms into
a twenty-foot globe, and suddenly the blob has edges. "Aim for that blob,"
Gulgrim yells, and the loaded guns go off together: Vadania's readied bow,
nineteen, five damage, a grunt from the leaves, stays up; Keechwah's readied
Witch Bolt, twenty-two, six lightning, a singe-hole through the bark — and the
figure drops out of the tree, hits the ground *on its feet*, and the dagger-light
shows the table what they've been fighting. A cloak blacker than the dark around
it. Midnight void, tailored. And on the chest, catching the light: an emblem.

**DM NOTE — INSPIRATION, INVENTED.** *First Heroic Inspiration I have ever
handed out, minted on the spot for throwing a lamp at the dark. The book says
reward heroism. The dwarf threw cutlery at a tree. Same thing. Minted.*

### Chapter 6 — We Out Here Having Lore

Vadania looks at the emblem and stops being in a combat. I send the image to the
channel — to Derek alone, private message, mid-initiative, while everyone
watches him read it — and what comes back across his face is the reason I run
this game. Burned village. He remembers. Does he ever forget the night his
village burned down? He remembers. Constitution save, dirty twenty: he takes the
memory, folds it, puts it away, keeps his turn. Then he dashes thirty feet
northeast, plants himself in front of a silent crouching void, and shouts the
only thing anyone has said to the enemy all night: "Who are you? Identify
yourself." Bow up. No action left to use it — the dash ate everything. Demand
first, ammunition later. The figure crouches lower. Says nothing. Somewhere
behind him, Derek says it for the table, quiet and exact: "We out here having
lore."

The rest of the round belongs to Chip, who is having the best worst night of
his life. Starry Wisp, seventeen, five damage plus anti-invisible light that
sticks to the figure until Chip's next turn — marked, glowing, no more hiding —
and then the bonus-action starry arrow, seventeen again, six more, and the void
is hurt, lit, and *still standing*, which tells you everything about what lives
inside that cloak. Shade's shortbow speaks from the bush — seventeen, twenty-four
with the sneak math, nine plus seven is sixteen — and now the figure is singed,
bolted, lit, and bleeding, and the table starts doing the thing tables do when
the math turns: leaning forward, counting, certain. Certainty, at my table, is
always premature. The forest has a second archer, and it has been waiting for
exactly this moment of certainty to spend it.

### Chapter 7 — Owlbear Justice

Twenty-two on Chip. Seven piercing, eight piercing, ten poison — twenty-five,
called out one number at a time the way executioners read sentences — and Chip
goes down to zero in the dirt. The starry light snuffs out like a stepped-on
candle. Death saves start — three rolls, three failures and he dies, three
successes and he holds — and the table does the quiet math that no one says
aloud: two failures and the druid dies in the mud while the bear watches. And
the bear — Water Bear, who failed every perception check all night, who sniffed
a nine and a ten and found nothing, who the table has been joking about for an
hour — puts *both paws* through the first marksman. Twenty-two for eight.
Fifteen for eleven. Eviscerated. Blood on the leaves, cloak in ribbons, silence
where the sniper was. The bear stands over the body breathing hard, and the
forest goes quiet except for dice, and Josh says nothing at all, because there
is nothing to say when your best friend does your avenging for you.

Triage, then, at speed. Where's the second shooter? Triangle ping says
northeast. Other tree, across the stream — not the first tree, a new one. Derek
says the fireball word and St3v3D says he doesn't care, he'll cast it, and Ben
says the line the transcript will keep forever — "there are no eyes in D, but
there are seven eyes in I don't care how big the room is, I cast Fireball" —
and then everybody remembers the rescue target is *somewhere* and stands down
from arson, which is growth, of a kind. Detect Magic, thirty feet: nothing.
Gulgrim runs the engine — Healing Word on Rocketcandy for ten mid-dash (a word
that heals, cast as a bonus alongside everything else), then
the long run to Chip, then Cure Wounds level two for sixteen (hands-on, bigger):
stable, no more
death saves, breathing — plus a bonus-action Healing Word on Keechwah for ten in
the same round, because James has decided healing is a full-time job with
overtime. Vadania double-dashes east. Chip's death save: fourteen. Alive.
Shade sixty feet to cover in one bound. Enemy nineteen on Keechwah: twenty
damage, seven and eight and five poison. Water Bear confirms northeast, moves
twenty feet, a furry compass. Gulgrim's second shift: Cure Wounds done, Healing
Word on Keechwah for ten. Status, called out like a hospital drama: Chip stable,
Keechwah lit but standing, Rocketcandy patched, Vadania closing, Shade hidden,
bear inbound. And then Chip — just revived, half movement, rolling at
disadvantage because he *died* six seconds ago, allowances made — sits up in the
dirt, squints northeast, and calls it: big tree, one-sixty-five to the middle,
one-twenty to the foliage. Moonbeam goes down, five-foot radius, Constitution
thirteen. Nothing illuminated. Nothing damaged. Nothing *visible*. Enemy thirteen
on Keechwah — armor twelve, it lands — eighteen plus seven poison, twenty-five.
Keechwah goes down in the dirt next to everything, and the forest, which has
been so loud all night, gets very, very quiet.

**DM NOTE — TRIAGE.** *Two down, one stable, one critical, one bear inbound, one
ranger forty feet out, one rogue in a bush, one paladin patched. I have run
combat for years and never called a round that sounded like an ER board. James
healed three people in six seconds and nobody thanked him because everyone was
busy bleeding. Medics: thanked never, needed always.*

### Chapter 8 — Where Is She?

The north tree shakes. Not wind — a scuffle, *inside* it, branches thrashing with
weight and intent. And then a black figure gets *hurled* out of the foliage onto
the rock face below: prone, winded, cloak tangled. A second figure lands after
it, standing, balanced, sword already at the first one's throat before the leaves
settle. And then the dagger-light finds the standing figure's face, and the
standing figure's clothes — studded leather, assassin-cut, moving without a
sound — and the table stops breathing, because the sword, the stance, the
silver-streaked hair all belong to someone who serves tepid water.

Biggins.

No butler suit. No tray. No manners. "Where is she? What have you done with
her?" The blade presses. A shallow cut, deliberate, non-lethal, professional —
the kind of cut that says *I know exactly where every vein is*. Back to the
throat. And then, to the captive, to the night, to the ring of battered
adventurers staring up at the boulder in the dagger-light: "I never should have
trusted a cut-rate brand of adventurers like you." And then he turns that
disgust on *us* — the whole gathered party, the rescuers, the victims of the
evening — and casts Vicious Mockery — one perfect insult, magically wounding,
four psychic damage — and the session ends.
Gulgrim gets a Healing Word onto Keechwah for eight just in time — timed to the
second, spared the insult by magic and reflexes — and the rest of us take the
four. All of it. Every point. We earned it. We left a girl alone at a festival
table, and her butler just told us what he thinks of that, in leather, with a
sword, and the dice are put away, and nobody is laughing, and that — *that* —
is where we stop.

**DM NOTE — THE REVIEW.** *Four psychic damage to the whole table as a
session-ender. I have ended sessions on dragon roars, on betrayals, on literal
cliffhangers. This is the first time I have ended one on a performance review.
He is right. We are cut-rate. See you next week.*
