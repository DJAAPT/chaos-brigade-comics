## 4. Strongest narrative moments (story engine of Journal 01)

1. **The forearm clasp (twice).** Rocketcandy's con works so well the guards clasp arms
   with him — then, after nearly arresting the party an hour later, *do it again*.
   The book's central visual irony: brotherhood ritual performed by extortionists.
2. **"THIEVES!!"** Sammy's eruption is the session's hinge: one cook's scream pivots
   the story from errand-day to guild conspiracy in four seconds.
3. **Shade's decode.** The quietest player names the whole plot (protection tax,
   outside the magistrate's purview) while everyone else argues about lunch money.
4. **Nat 1 → 17.** Vadania's humiliation-to-Keechwah's-triumph at the job board is the
   party dynamic in miniature: fail loudly, get carried quietly.
5. **The pan.** A shopkeeper ends a three-guard shakedown with cookware while the
   druid narrates his own leg sweep. Best action scene in the session, no contest.
6. **Dead air where magic should be.** Keechwah's empty hand + unheard whisper is the
   only scene with genuine dread — and it pays off nothing yet, which is the hook.
7. **"Pretend that you have taste."** Four psychic damage as a punchline; the butler
   wins the only fight that happens in a mansion.
8. **The downtime journals.** The cold open of who these people *think* they are
   (legend / not-a-pawn / scholar / stonehenge enthusiast) vs. who the session proves
   them to be. Ben's framing device for the whole journal format.

## 5. Ben's narrator voice (as developed for this book)

First person, over-a-drink. Sarcastic but never cruel; exasperated but never
contemptuous — the affection is the joke's license. Operating rules:

- **Knows the plan, reports the wreckage.** Ben constantly contrasts prep vs. outcome
  ("I had three outcomes prepared. They found a fourth."), but never blames — the
  wreckage delights him.
- **Dice as weather, not math.** Rolls enter as fate events ("the die came up one,
  and the board filled with jobs nobody wants"), with the number stated once and
  moved past. No mechanics lectures; a first-timer learns d20 logic by osmosis.
- **Roasts are earned, never random.** Every jab traces to something the player
  actually said or did. Running gags compound (clasps, "we're good," taste).
- **Own mistakes on the table.** VTT chaos, the d4 ruling, the Bob Barker derail —
  Ben narrates his own fumbles as readily as theirs. This is what makes him a host,
  not a heckler.
- **Never purple, never crowded.** Short declarative sentences, one image per beat,
  dialogue doing the lifting. Funny lines are the players'; Ben's job is setup and
  timing.

## 8. SAMPLE — Chapters 1–3 (new narrative, transcript-sourced)

### Chapter 1 — The Downtime Diaries

A month passes between adventures, and a responsible Dungeon Master asks what
everyone was doing. A responsible party answers with journals. I have kept these
journals. I have *read* these journals. What follows is condensed, because one of
them is titled "Chronicles of Rocket Candy" and it is exactly as long as you fear.

Rocketcandy spent the month on cliff duty, hurling stirges like javelins off the
observatory rocks, and reports all of this the way other people report the weather.
Eighteen armor class — how hard he is to hit, for the civilians — and stayed, in his words, "to become a legend." He then describes
exiting a cave trailing marine layer behind him purely, and I quote, "because I
enjoy the aesthetic of walking out of the mist like a storm made flesh." I did not
ask him to read that part aloud. Nobody can stop him from reading things aloud.

Shade's contribution is titled "absolutely not diary of shade," which tells you most
of what you need, and describes a coin landing on its edge, the wind pausing, and an
unseen hand moving pieces across some board. His conclusion: "I do not intend to be
a pawn." Four sentences. The man rations words like trail rations, and every one of
them is doing threat assessment.

Gulgrim stayed on the isle making potions and meditating on what it means to heal
and protect people, which is the single healthiest month anyone in this party has
ever had, and nobody has tried to replicate it since.

Keechwah went to the archives. Bargained with traveling sages — a horned tiefling
warlock, skin an unnatural red-brown the table has agreed not to stare at. Hunched over
candlelight comparing arcane theories, testing the pact magic instead of merely
wielding it — a warlock's power comes through a patron, an otherworldly being he has
a deal with, which is the whole arrangement — recording everything in a growing grimoire. His thesis, boiled down:
true power is constructed, not stumbled upon. It is the most disciplined thing
anyone at my table has ever said, and I need you to remember that, because within
the week he will be staring at his own empty hand while his god ignores him.

Chip left the isle early — for Neverwinter, for the stars. Star-maps. Beginner
stonehenges, dragged into place by Water Bear — Albert on the bad days, same bear,
and nobody has ever gotten an explanation. Suspicious rope and horse tack he
declines to explain. Chip's months are indistinguishable from his days, which is
either enlightenment or the druid lifestyle, and I have stopped trying to tell the
difference.

**DM NOTE — PREPARATION STATUS: the ferry.** *One boat ride. Six journals. I had
budgeted ten minutes for the crossing. It took forty. I have started budgeting
for who these people are instead of what I planned.*

### Chapter 2 — Ten Gold to Walk In

Neverwinter holds thirty to forty thousand people, and charges ten gold a head to
look at them. The party's ferry docks at midday. The guards want sixty gold. The
party has just enough history with money to find this offensive.

Rocketcandy steps forward. "Well, excuse me, guards. Have you guys not heard of
the legendary Rocketcandy yet?"

Reader: I need you to understand something about Dungeons & Dragons. When a player
tries something like this, they roll a twenty-sided die, add a bonus, and the total
decides whether the world cooperates. This was, by any honest measure, an absurd
thing to attempt on six armed tax collectors. Andrew rolled an eighteen, plus four:
twenty-two. The world cooperated.

The guards knew his *name*. Knew his *family*. One of them clasped forearms with
him, and Rocketcandy — who did not remember this man at all — returned the gesture
with total confidence, because that is what you do when you are the protagonist of
the Chronicles of yourself. ("I returned the gesture. I don't remember him, but I
returned the gesture." Verbatim. I keep it framed.)

Meanwhile Shade is doing what Shade does, which is looking at the thing nobody
mentioned. Of six guards, one or two bear a small sigil inked inside the wrist.
Shade clocks it. Ben files it. The tax collector has it.

**DM NOTE — THINGS I LEARNED.** *Tattoo the conspiracy onto the toll booth and the
rogue WILL find it in eleven minutes. Do not tattoo anything you are not prepared
to explain before lunch.*

Mid-ferry, a sixth player arrives. Derek — that's the player; Vadania, wood elf
ranger, is the character, and I will use whichever name the moment deserves — joins the
table nineteen minutes into the session having missed the entire adventure to date,
and covers it the way you cover a late arrival to a tavern: by announcing he has
been training for revenge, alone, for his family, and is now, finally, peppy. "Since
I met you guys, I've been pretty peppy in spirits. Now that I finally feel like I
have some friends and been a loner for such a long time, it's nice to have you guys
around." The party adopts him instantly. Loneliness is the one backstory nobody at
this table is willing to fail a check against. Chip meets the ferry, hugs nobody,
and peels off for supplies on the eastern edge of town — he will rejoin this story
through the back door of a general store, and it will not go well.

Then they walk into my city, past the dragonborn and the goliaths and the market
crowds, and tell the innkeeper absolutely everything. But that is the next chapter.

### Chapter 3 — Marigold's (first half; sample ends at the scream)

Marigold's Inn is the kind of place adventurers dream about between dungeons: low
beams, candlelight, a hostess built entirely out of welcome. Marigold herself is a
halfling innkeeper with tiny curls and a warm rosy face, shorter than her own bar —
the party realizes she is a halfling the way you realize a door is locked, by
walking directly into the fact. She seats six armed weirdos and an owlbear's
reputation, recites sandwiches, soups, and roasted meats from memory, and asks for
their story.

 Reader, they give it. All of it. Vadania — nineteen minutes into knowing these
people — announces, "We just happen to be here," and, and I quote the table,
they "proceed to tell her our entire story of our adventures." Operational
security: zero. Appetizers: excellent. Rocketcandy orders roast meat and potato
and wine and goes terse, which for him means brooding. Keechwah orders something
distilled from a potato and something bubbly, and at one point informs the table
that his partner seems "a little dark complected" and the bones should be left
down, a sentence I have replayed four times and understand less with each pass.
Derek gets ale and chicken. Shade gets whatever she recommends, because Shade has
never once in his life ordered wrong.

Around them, the room talks: merchants in from the south with stories about the
weather turning sideways for no reason, minstrels complaining it is unseasonably
warm, tide gossip, pockets of chatter. I laid all of this out like a dessert tray.
Nobody touched it. They were too busy telling a civilian innkeeper about the time
they fought a dragon.

Vadania says the word "dragon."

Marigold screams.

The whole room startles. Chairs scrape. And Ben, who has run halflings for years,
watches his halfling innkeeper explain between gasps that her people do *not* have
positive experiences with dragons, that they quite terrify her, and then — because
the party has mentioned mushroom people in passing — sit in absolute awe of the
revelation that mushroom people *exist*. "How? What could possibly be next? My
heart!" she says, hands to her chest, and I want you to picture it: a three-foot
woman who has just learned two impossible things before dessert, holding her heart
like it might file a complaint.

Vadania, to his credit, is asked what the party is even doing, and answers, more
or less: "Good question. We have no idea. Right now we're just eating. Well, I'm
just eating some shit."

## 14. Approaches considered (my recommendation: B)

- **A. Memoir-in-order.** Strict chronology, Ben retrospective throughout. Cheapest,
  but wastes the cold-open hook and the transcript's best surprise (the clasp means
  more once you know the guild).
- **B. Cold-open + told-back (RECOMMENDED).** Open on the docks clasp, rewind to
  downtime, tell forward. Uses the session's own dramatic irony; the sample above is
  written in this mode. Costs one structural device, earns the hook.
- **C. Interleaved threads.** Cut party/mansion against Chip/store as parallel
  chapters throughout. Truest to the timeline, but the transcript's gap [1:24–1:30]
  forces invention at the join — violates the no-invention rule. Rejected; Chip's
  thread runs as one block chapter (Ch. 7), framed as "meanwhile" in Ben's voice.
